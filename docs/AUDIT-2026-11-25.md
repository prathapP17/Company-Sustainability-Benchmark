# Repository audit, 25 November 2026

Starting point: `main` at `08d430d`, clean working tree, all seven brief phases done. The audit read the code, not the
README. Findings are grouped below. **Fixed** means the improvement work after this audit changed it; everything else is
listed under "open" at the end.

## What exists and works

- Curated JSON company files (10 companies), zod schema plus cross-field validator, rebuilt SQLite read model
  (`benchmark.db`), operational database (`pipeline.db`, additive migrations, backup before each migration).
- Public pages: dashboard, company profiles, compare, peer groups, targets (SBTi pace check), Scope 3 heatmap, industry
  KPIs, transparency checklist, rankings, methodology, downloads (CSV, XLSX, PDF).
- Review pipeline: upload, text extraction (PDF, XLSX, XHTML, offline OCR), rule-based extractor, optional Claude
  extractor, evidence gate, conflict and restatement detection, approval writing to the JSON files, audit log.
- Discovery of new reports, scheduled refresh, alerts; accounts with roles, sessions, throttling, lockout, CSRF origin
  check; Docker/Caddy configuration; snapshot backups and a restore drill; AI summaries with a citation check; GDELT
  news with reviewer approval.
- 133 unit tests, two browser suites with axe-core, performance budgets, extraction evaluation, CI.

## Bugs and weaknesses found

### Analytical integrity (most important)

1. **Peer groups mix industries.** `peerGroup` is the only industry key used for analysis. Toyota (Automobiles, TR-AU)
   and Siemens (Industrial Machinery / Electrical Equipment) share `autos_industrials`; Nestlé (Processed Foods) and
   Unilever (Household & Personal Products) share `consumer_staples`. Peer medians, "rank X of N in group" and
   industry-metric applicability all used this broad group.
2. **Industry metrics assigned by broad group.** `au_bev_sales` applied to Siemens; consumer-staples packaging metrics
   were not tied to the SASB industries that define them.
3. **The UI warned but the median still computed.** `compareSet()` returned "caution" for different consolidation
   approaches while `peerMedian()` still produced a median from the same values. The two used different rules.
4. **Peer medians ignored reporting periods.** Values from different fiscal years (FY2024 next to FY2025, or periods
   ending in different calendar years) entered one median.
5. **No single eligibility decision.** Comparison table, chart, peer median, performance indicators and rankings each
   had their own subset of checks (`rankable`, `compareSet`, ad-hoc filters).
6. **Year-on-year performance indicators** did not check that both years used the same consolidation approach, boundary
   or Scope 2 method.
7. **Transparency item "SASB industry metric disclosed"** counted any industry metric, not one that applies to the
   company's own SASB industry, and scored companies whose industry has no metric in the dictionary as "not met"
   instead of "not assessable".
8. **Rankings showed "N of M in <broad group>"**, implying peers that are not industry peers.
9. Dashboard copy was stale ("Phase 1 … comparisons come in phase 2").

### Permissions (against brief section 7: guest < viewer < reviewer < admin)

10. **Viewers could read reviewer and admin material:** the review queue, uploaded documents, discovered reports, alerts,
    news and summary queues, and the **audit log** (an admin record in the brief).
11. **Downloads were public**, although the brief gives exports and reports to viewers.

### AI summaries

12. The checker verified numbers and banned some words, but did not check that a sentence's claim matches its facts:
    trend words on a single-year value, the direction of a change, causation, target-achievement claims, metric names
    (Scope 1 vs Scope 3), units, year/value pairing, boundary qualifiers, gap wording, and "reported" used for derived
    values all passed. Failed summaries were discarded with no manual-review path.

### Extraction

13. Measured on 8 curated source reports (184 gold values): recall 36.4%, judged precision 44.4%. Main causes found by
    reading the per-value results: several distinct numbers proposed for one metric/year (footnote markers, years,
    adjacent cells), whole documents read at 0% (Siemens, Unilever), and no separate measures for wrong year, unit,
    boundary or Scope 2 method.
14. Ambiguous rows were skipped silently; nothing recorded them as gaps for the reviewer.

### Review queue

15. Statuses existed (`pending`, `blocked`, `approved`, `rejected`, `superseded`, evidence `verified`/`failed`,
    conflict `duplicate`/`conflict`) but low confidence, possible duplicate and possible restatement were not shown as
    distinct, filterable states.

### UI and UX

16. Ten top-level links in one flat row; review area not reachable from the navigation; no search.
17. No map; the dashboard was three long tables.
18. Dark mode followed the system only, with no way to choose.

### Performance

19. Pages computed comparability per row inside render; acceptable at 10 companies. Recharts is loaded only on the Scope 3
    page. No real bottleneck at current size; noted for growth.

### Deployment

20. Docker build and compose stack never run (no Docker on the development Mac). Still true after this work.

### Tests

21. No tests for per-role permissions of each review page and route, the comparability engine, period checks, the
    industry-metric rule, or adversarial AI summaries.

## Data integrity rules checked and found intact

Four disclosure states (never collapsed; CHECK constraint in SQLite), source and page required for disclosed values,
boundary/consolidation/Scope 2/assurance/verification class stored, conflict detection and explicit restatements with
history, evidence gate before approval, proposals only from automation, additive migrations with backups, tests in
temporary directories, secrets git-ignored.
